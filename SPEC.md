# SPEC.md — Remote Doctor

> Product specification for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 (St. John's, Nov 14–15).
> Challenge: **Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions**.
> Read this together with CLAUDE.md. If they conflict, the official challenge text wins.

---

## 1. One-line idea

**Remote Doctor helps an astronaut on a long mission check their own health every day and know the one thing to do next — even when the doctor on Earth is 20 minutes away.**

## 2. The problem

- Long missions expose astronauts to NASA's **5 hazards**: space radiation, isolation and confinement, distance from Earth, gravity fields (altered gravity), and hostile/closed environments.
- These cause immune changes, bone loss, heart and vessel changes, eye/brain changes (SANS), poor sleep, nutrition problems and mental health problems.
- On deep-space missions, Mission Control cannot help in real time (Mars: about 3–22 minutes one-way delay, plus blackouts).
- Astronauts are busy and tired, and often say "I'm fine."
- So the crew must **assess themselves** and **know when to escalate**.

## 3. User

- **Primary:** the astronaut (not a doctor, not Mission Control).
- **Secondary:** the flight surgeon on Earth, who reads reports later (asynchronous).
- Assume the user is tired, busy, and not a medical expert. Explain everything simply.

## 4. Design principles (non-negotiable)

1. **Glanceable first.** Status and action first. Raw numbers only on tap.
2. **Every color has a reason and an action.** Never a color alone.
3. **Offline-first.** Everything runs in the browser. No backend. No network calls.
4. **Latency-aware.** Earth is far. Messages are queued and arrive late.
5. **Calm technology.** No alarm noise, no clutter, no dashboards full of charts.
6. **Big touch targets.** Usable with gloves or in a hurry (min 48px).
7. **Not a medical device.** Show a small disclaimer: "Prototype for education. Not a medical device."

## 5. Features

### Tier 1 — MUST build (Saturday)

#### F1. Simulated crew
- 3 fictional crew members on a **Mars transit**, **Day 140 of 210** (landing day = Day 210).
- Each has a personal **baseline** (their own normal values).
- Data is generated by **physiology models** (see section 7). **No `Math.random()`.** Use a seeded noise function for small natural variation.
- Clearly labelled: **"Simulated crew data."**

| Crew | Role | Demo story |
|---|---|---|
| Cmdr. Maya Okafor | Commander | Skipping exercise + sleep debt + early SANS signs (headache, blurry near vision). The main demo story. |
| Dr. Lucas Tremblay | Crew Medical Officer (Canadian) | Higher radiation dose after a simulated solar particle event. |
| Sam Ito | Flight Engineer | Stable. Shows what "doing well" looks like. |

#### F2. Clickable Body Map ("Ghost Self") — signature feature
- A body outline (SVG) showing the astronaut **on landing day** (projected).
- Healthy areas glow steady; at-risk areas dim or flicker softly.
- **Each body part is clickable.** The detail panel shows:
  1. **What it does** (one simple sentence)
  2. **Status now** + **trend** (improving / stable / worsening)
  3. **Which hazard** affects it (from the 5 hazards)
  4. **How it is measured in space** (simple)
  5. **Trend chart** over mission days (small, simple)
  6. **What to do** (action + escalation level)
- Body parts:

| Body part | Main risk | Hazard | Indicators used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brain / mind | Mood, stress, motion sickness | Isolation, gravity | Mood, stress, symptoms |
| Eyes | SANS (headward fluid shift) | Gravity | Headache, blurry near vision, weekly eye check |
| Heart & vessels | Vessels stiffen, orthostatic intolerance on landing | Gravity, radiation | Heart rate, blood pressure |
| Blood / immune | Anemia, weaker immunity, dormant virus reactivation | Closed environment, stress | Body temperature, symptoms (rash, fatigue) |
| Muscles | Mass and strength loss | Gravity | Exercise minutes, strength test |
| Bones | Density loss, fracture risk | Gravity | Exercise minutes, mission day (model) |
| Sleep (whole body) | Sleep loss, body-clock drift | Isolation, schedule | Sleep hours, sleep quality |
| Metabolism | Insulin resistance | Gravity, inactivity | Blood sugar (mg/dL) |
| Radiation (whole-body layer) | Cancer, cataract risk | Radiation | Cumulative dose (mSv) |

#### F3. Morning check-in (about 30 seconds)
- Overlay with big tap buttons. Minimal typing.
- Collects the **6 NASA-suggested indicators**: blood pressure, heart rate, blood sugar, body temperature, sleep hours, mood.
- Plus space-specific: headache / blurry vision (SANS), exercise minutes yesterday, any symptoms (rash, dizziness, nausea).
- Number inputs come pre-filled with the simulated sensor value; the astronaut confirms or edits.
- Saves to local storage. Updates the Body Map, forecast and action immediately.

#### F4. Landing-day forecast
- For each body part: a short forecast in plain words, e.g. "Bones: slow decline. At this rate, fracture risk is elevated on landing day."
- Each forecast shows: **trend**, **reason**, **landing-day meaning**.
- Uses forecast words (e.g. Steady / Drifting / Declining / Recovering), not just red/yellow/green.

#### F5. One Action + escalation level
- One clear action for today (the highest-priority one), with **why** and **how**.
- Example: "Wear thigh cuffs for 4 hours today. Why: your headaches + blurry near vision suggest fluid shift toward the head (SANS). Thigh cuffs keep fluid in the legs."
- Every finding gets one **escalation level**:

| Level | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Self-care | Handle it yourself. Follow the steps. |
| 2 | Crew Medical Officer | Show a crewmate today. |
| 3 | Flight Surgeon | Added to the Earth report (queued, answer comes later). |
| 4 | Urgent protocol | Act now. Do not wait for Earth. Step-by-step protocol shown. |

- Escalation rules must use **context**: personal baseline, trend over several days, and combinations of signs — not a single threshold.

#### F6. Earth link bar
- Always visible at the top: one-way delay, link status, next sync window.
- Example: "Earth · 18 min one-way · Link closed · Next sync 14:20 (in 52 min)."
- Shows "X reports waiting in queue."

### Tier 2 — build Sunday morning (only if Tier 1 works)

#### F7. Earth Report (daily + weekly)
- Auto-built from local data.
- Top line: **"What the doctor should look at first."**
- Contents: forecast per body part with trend, check-in answers, actions done/skipped, cumulative radiation dose, open escalations.
- Reports wait in a **queue** and are "sent" at the next sync window.
- A simulated **flight surgeon reply** arrives later (after the round-trip delay). Example: "Agree with thigh cuffs. Add weekly eye check (OCT). — Dr. Hart, Flight Surgeon."

#### F8. "How to measure" mini-guides
- For each indicator: 3–4 simple steps, why it matters in space, and when to escalate.
- Because NASA says: do not assume astronauts know everything.

#### F9. Environment switch
- Presets: **Space Station (LEO)**, **Mars transit**, **Moon base**, **Mars base**.
- Changes: comm delay, gravity level, radiation rate, sync windows.

#### F10. "What if" slider (nice to have)
- Example: "What if I skip exercise for 14 days?" → the Ghost Self and forecast update live.

### Tier 3 — slides only (do NOT build)
- Camera images / ultrasound input.
- Real wearable sensors (ARCHeR wrist device, Astroskin smart shirt).
- Full multi-patient doctor portal.
- Heavy gamification.

## 6. Environments (config)

> Approximate values for simulation. Verify before quoting in the pitch.

| Environment | One-way delay to Earth | Gravity | Radiation dose rate (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Space Station (LEO) | near real-time | ~0 g (microgravity) | ~0.5 mSv/day |
| Moon base | ~1.3 s | 0.17 g | ~1.4 mSv/day (Chang'e-4 LND, Zhang et al. 2020) |
| Mars transit (default) | 3–22 min (demo: 18 min) | ~0 g | ~1.8 mSv/day |
| Mars base | 3–22 min | 0.38 g | ~0.7 mSv/day |

## 7. Simulation models (`src/sim/`)

Rules:
- Deterministic functions of **mission day**, **behaviour** (exercise, sleep, countermeasures) and **events** (e.g. solar particle event).
- Small variation via a **seeded** noise function (same seed = same data every run).
- Every rate is a named constant with a comment saying where it comes from or "assumption".

| Model | Simple behaviour | Source / note |
|---|---|---|
| Bone density | Loses up to ~1–1.5% per month in weightlessness; resistance exercise slows the loss | CSA infographic |
| Muscle strength | Declines with low exercise; recovers with exercise | Assumption, tuned for demo |
| Fluid shift / SANS score | Rises slowly with mission days; thigh cuffs reduce it; drives headache + blurry vision | NASA HRP SANS risk; Thigh Cuff study |
| Sleep debt | Builds when sleep < personal need (~8 h); body-clock drift with irregular schedule | NASA HRP sleep risk |
| Heart & vessels | Resting heart rate and blood pressure shift with deconditioning; landing-day orthostatic risk | NASA HRP cardiovascular risk |
| Immune | Stress + sleep debt raise the chance of symptoms (rash, fatigue) | NASA HRP immune risk |
| Metabolism | Inactivity slowly raises fasting blood sugar | CSA infographic (insulin resistance) |
| Radiation | Cumulative dose = daily rate × days + event spikes; ~30% neutrons (ISS figure) | CSA "Monitoring radiation" |
| Mood / stress | Isolation + sleep debt lower mood over time | NASA HRP behavioural risk |

## 8. Data model (`src/types.ts`) — outline

- `CrewMember`: id, name, role, baseline values, seed.
- `Mission`: environment, currentDay, landingDay, delayMinutes, syncWindows.
- `DailyRecord`: day, indicators (HR, BP, glucose, temp, sleepHours, mood), symptoms, exerciseMinutes, countermeasures, radiationDose.
- `BodyPartStatus`: part, status, trend, reason, hazard, landingDayForecast, escalationLevel.
- `Action`: title, why, how, escalationLevel, offlineProtocol.
- `EarthReport`: type (daily/weekly), createdAt, summaryFirstLine, sections, queueState (queued/sent/replied), reply.

## 9. Architecture

```
SIMULATION (sim/)  → realistic body data, day by day
      ↓
ENGINE (engine/)   → forecasts, escalation levels, One Action
      ↓
STORE (store/)     → app state, saved in localStorage (offline)
      ↓
SCREENS (components/) → what the astronaut sees

REPORTS (reports/) → build reports, queue them, sync at windows, delayed reply
```

Folder structure:

```
src/
  main.tsx, App.tsx, types.ts
  data/       crew.ts, mission.ts, environments.ts, bodyParts.ts, guides.ts
  sim/        physiology.ts, simulate.ts, noise.ts
  engine/     forecast.ts, escalation.ts, actions.ts
  reports/    buildReport.ts, earthQueue.ts
  store/      useAppStore.ts
  components/ EarthLinkBar.tsx, BodyMap.tsx, BodyPartPanel.tsx, CheckIn.tsx,
              ForecastList.tsx, ActionCard.tsx, ReportView.tsx, TrendChart.tsx
  styles/     theme.css
```

## 10. UI layout

- **No left sidebar. No generic admin template.**
- One main screen ("Today"):
  - Top: Earth link bar + crew switcher + mission day ("Day 140 / 210 · 70 days to landing").
  - Center: Body Map (large).
  - Side panel: selected body part details (or forecast list when nothing is selected).
  - Bottom: One Action card.
- Check-in: full-screen overlay, opened by a big "Morning check-in" button.
- Report: a second view, switched with a simple top toggle (Today / Earth Report).
- Look: dark, calm "spacecraft cabin" theme. One accent color. Clear type. Subtle animation only.

## 11. Tech

- Vite + React + TypeScript.
- Charts: Recharts (small trend charts only).
- Body Map: hand-made SVG.
- State: React state + a small store hook, persisted to localStorage.
- Hosting: Vercel (auto-deploy from GitHub `main`).
- No backend, no database, no external API calls.

## 12. Build order

1. Types + data (crew, mission, environments, body parts).
2. Simulation (sim/) + quick test that values look realistic.
3. Engine (forecast, escalation, actions).
4. Body Map + detail panel.
5. Earth link bar + One Action card.
6. Morning check-in.
7. **(Sunday)** Earth Report + queue + delayed reply.
8. **(Sunday)** Mini-guides, environment switch, what-if slider.
9. **Sunday 12:00 — feature freeze.** Only bug fixes and polish after this.

## 13. Demo story (about 60 seconds)

"Day 140 of 210 on the way to Mars. Commander Maya is tired. She skipped exercise and says she's fine. Watch her Ghost Self: her eyes and bones are dimming. Tap the eyes — early signs of SANS. Remote Doctor gives her one action: thigh cuffs, and a check with the crew medical officer. The report goes into the queue for Earth. Eighteen minutes later, the flight surgeon agrees. On Mars, the doctor is far away. Remote Doctor is right there."
